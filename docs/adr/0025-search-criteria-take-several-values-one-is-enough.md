# Search Criteria take several values, and meeting one is enough

A Profile's Search Criteria held one location, one contract type and one remote-work option, while Candidates over 40 often look in two regions, or for a permanent post or a freelance mission alike. We decided that locations (up to five), contract types and remote-work options each take several values, the target role and the minimum salary staying single: a Profile is one positioning, and another role is another Profile.

- **Match Score:** a Job Offer meets a criterion as soon as it meets one of its values (CDI or Freelance wanted, the offer is in CDI: a match; near one of the locations: a match). The weights do not change. None chosen still means no preference.
- **Job discovery:** one search per location (France Travail filters by one area at a time), with the contract types and remote-work options together in each query; the Job discovery budget (Job Offers, euros, seconds) is shared between the locations, never multiplied, so a Job Search costs what it did.
- **Job Searches for all Profiles:** the Recherches d'offres page starts a Job Search for one Profile or for every active Profile at once, one Job Search (and one use of the Plan Quota) each, and shows the results grouped by Profile, in tabs or in swimlanes.
- **Entering locations:** places are chosen from geo.api.gouv.fr suggestions (communes, départements, régions) or typed, but stored as plain strings, so Match Score and Job discovery are unchanged.
- Job Offers keep one value per field: they say what one posting states.

## Considered Options

- **One search naming every location:** cheaper, but France Travail cannot filter by several areas and web search results drift to the first place named.
- **Several target roles in one Profile:** it would blur the Master CV and Match Score a Profile carries; a second role is a second Profile.
- **Every value must match:** no Job Offer is both CDI and freelance; "one is enough" is what the Candidate means.

Addendum (#236): Search Criteria also take Sectors, Subsectors and Domains, several of each and all optional, like contract types: a Sector is a section of INSEE's NAF rév. 2 (A to U), a Subsector one of its divisions (two digits), a Domain a ROME 4.0 professional domain of France Travail (M13). They are kept as codes; the codes the Search Criteria accept, and which Sector each Subsector belongs to, are fixed in the code, while their labels are Reference data, fetched without a key from INSEE's SPARQL endpoint (French and English) and France Travail's ROME open data (French), kept in the database, read on first need and refreshed monthly by the worker, a failed source keeping its labels. Choosing a Subsector chooses its Sector; only the chosen Sectors' Subsectors are offered. They steer Job discovery only, and the Match Score weights do not change: the France Travail search filters by `secteurActivite` (the Subsectors chosen, plus every Subsector of a Sector chosen without any) and `domaine` (the Domains), comma-separated like `typeContrat`, both still unverified against a live call (docs/research/issue-236.md); web search queries name the Sectors by their labels (the Subsectors' when some are chosen), never by a code and never the Domains, so they stay within ADR-0007's Search Criteria. Profiles and API clients from before default to empty lists. NAF 2025 replaces NAF rév. 2 in January 2027; the fixed codes will then need migrating. We rejected the authenticated ROME API (a second subscription, 1 call per second) for its open data, the 14 grand domains for the 110 professional domains, and comparing Sectors in the Match Score (a Job Offer's sector is free text the AI reads, not a NAF code).
